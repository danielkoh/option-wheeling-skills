# Bill Ackman (Pershing Square) — Concentrated Quality + Activism

Ackman runs a **highly concentrated** book (often ~8–12 names) of **simple, predictable,
free-cash-flow-generative, high-quality businesses**, bought at a reasonable price and held
for years — sometimes with an **activist catalyst** to unlock value. Think "Buffett-style
quality, but concentrated, catalyzed, and occasionally adversarial."

## Core principles
1. **Business quality first.** He wants **simple, predictable, durable** businesses he can
   forecast far out — dominant franchises with pricing power (e.g. Chipotle, Hilton, QSR
   royalty models, Howard Hughes). "Capital-light, high-return, dominant."
2. **Free cash flow & high ROIC.** Capital-light compounders that throw off cash and reinvest
   at high returns; recurring/royalty/contractual revenue preferred over cyclical/commodity.
3. **Predictability.** He explicitly avoids businesses whose future he can't model — no deep
   cyclicals, no commodity price-takers, no balance-sheet-driven black boxes.
4. **Concentration with conviction.** A handful of positions, deeply researched. Few decisions,
   made well, sized big. Requires being *right* and *patient*.
5. **Reasonable price.** Quality at a fair (not any) price; demands a margin of safety, often
   buying when a temporary problem depresses a great business.
6. **Activism as an optional catalyst.** When management/strategy/capital-allocation is the
   gap, he engages: board seats, strategic/operational change, capital return, spin-offs,
   cost structure. The catalyst converts intrinsic value into realized value. (He also makes
   asymmetric **macro hedges** — e.g. credit/rates — separate from the equity book.)

## What Ackman looks for
- Dominant, **simple** business with a long runway and **pricing power**.
- **High ROIC, strong FCF conversion, recurring revenue**, manageable leverage.
- A **specific catalyst** or fixable problem (mismanagement, hidden assets, bad capital
  allocation) creating an asymmetric setup.
- Management he can partner with — or replace.

## Red flags (Ackman avoids)
- Complexity / unpredictability he can't underwrite; deep cyclicals & commodities.
- Over-levered balance sheets; financials whose risk is opaque.
- No catalyst and no path to one (a cheap-but-stuck business can stay stuck).
- Businesses where activism can't move the needle.

## Quantitative checks (what `ackman_screen` encodes)
- **FCF margin ≥ ~15%**; high FCF conversion of earnings.
- **ROIC ≥ ~15%** (capital-light compounder).
- **Predictable/recurring revenue** (high revenue-predictability score).
- **Interest coverage ≥ ~4×** (sound balance sheet).

## How to apply
Confirm the business is **simple, predictable, FCF-generative, high-ROIC**; value it as a
compounder (DCF/owner-earnings with a long runway); identify the **catalyst** (operational,
capital-allocation, or activist) and the asymmetric payoff; pressure-test concentration risk
("would I size this 10–15% of a book?") and what makes the thesis wrong.
