# Warren Buffett — Quality at a Fair Price

Buffett (with Munger) evolved from Graham's "cigar-butt" deep value to **buying wonderful
businesses at fair prices and holding "forever."** The lens is *business quality + durable
moat + honest, able management + a sensible price.*

## Core principles
1. **Circle of competence.** Only invest in businesses you can understand and whose earnings
   you can forecast a decade out. "Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing." If you
   can't model the business simply, pass — it's not a defect, it's discipline.
2. **Durable competitive advantage (the "moat").** Sustainable pricing power that protects
   high returns on capital from competition. Sources:
   - **Brand / intangibles** (Coca-Cola, See's — people pay up).
   - **Switching costs** (entrenched software, banks).
   - **Network effects.**
   - **Cost advantages / scale** (GEICO, low-cost producers).
   - **Regulatory / efficient-scale** (utilities, railroads — BNSF).
   Test: could a well-funded competitor take share? If yes, the moat is thin.
3. **Honest, capable management** that thinks like owners and allocates capital well. Read
   the proxy: insider ownership, comp aligned with per-share value, candor about mistakes,
   no serial dilution or empire-building M&A.
4. **Consistent, predictable earnings** with high returns on equity/capital achieved
   **without excessive leverage.**
5. **Margin of safety** — buy at a discount to conservative intrinsic value. Patience: it's
   fine to wait years for the price ("no called strikes" — you only swing at fat pitches).
6. **Long horizon.** "Our favorite holding period is forever." Let compounding work; minimize
   turnover, taxes, and frictional costs.

## Quantitative checks (what a mechanical screen encodes)
- **ROE ≥ ~15%** consistently (durable returns, not one good year).
- **Gross margin ≥ ~40%** (proxy for pricing power) and stable/rising.
- **Low leverage**: Debt/Equity ≤ ~0.5; comfortable interest coverage.
- **FCF margin ≥ ~10%**; earnings backed by cash; high **owner earnings**.
- **Earnings consistency**: up in most years over a decade; resilient in recessions.
- Value via **owner earnings** discounted at a conservative rate (he uses a long-bond-like
  hurdle, not a beta-driven CAPM — he rejects beta as a risk measure).

## Red flags (Buffett passes)
- Business he can't understand or predict (much of early-stage tech, fad-driven names).
- Reliance on continual leverage or dilution to grow.
- Commodity business with no pricing power and poor returns on capital.
- Management that overpromises, games accounting, or makes ego-driven acquisitions.
- A wonderful business at an *unwonderful* price — great company ≠ great stock at any price.

## How to apply
State whether it's in your circle of competence, name the moat (and stress-test its
durability), grade management/capital allocation, confirm high ROE/ROIC + low leverage +
strong owner earnings, then compare price to a conservative intrinsic value and demand a
margin of safety. Quote his framing: *"It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair
price than a fair company at a wonderful price."*
